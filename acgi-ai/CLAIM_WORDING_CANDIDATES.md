## Recommendation

**HERO**
> Your AI agent's default is to act on an unclear instruction — not to stop and ask.

**SUBHEAD**
> Answer a few questions about one task — what it touches, what it can reach,
> whether it can be undone, which regulated domain it falls under — and a
> governance brief comes back in your browser: the risk level, the safer
> operating mode to run it in, the boundaries to set, when to stop, and the
> GDPR, HIPAA, or PCI obligations to consider for that task. It is decision
> guidance, not a runtime guard on your agent — and not legal advice.

**CTA**
> Start the governance interview  → `#interview` (anchor ships: `Marketing.tsx:1319`)

### Why this wording (base + grafts + drops)

- **Base:** the `plain-clarity` candidate — the only screen-clean, ship-eligible
  entry (scored 8 / 9 / 8 persuasiveness / truthfulness / claim-safety). Kept its
  spine: concrete second person, in-browser mechanism, brief fields named one-for-one.
- **Graft 1 — failure-mode hook** (from `risk-first`, in plain language): leads with
  the recognizable failure the product exists to correct — an agent's default is to
  act on ambiguity, not ask. Describes the **agent's tendency**, not a runtime claim
  about the product. Maps to shipped `riskSignals` / `failureModes` and the interview
  heading "Classify the task before the agent acts".
- **Graft 2 — named regulated axis** (from `outcome-first`, now de-risked): names
  GDPR / HIPAA / PCI as **"obligations to consider"** (the shipped framing), never as
  satisfied / met / attested. This is the dimension the first (contaminated) run wrongly omitted.
- **Drops** (real overclaims caught by the screen): `exactly` (unbackable precision
  promise), `two-minute` (no such benchmark in source — grep returned zero), and the
  dense URL-param mechanic (true, but one loose phrasing turns it into a live-surface overclaim).
- **Added defense:** "decision guidance, not a runtime guard on your agent" + "not legal
  advice" — closes the gap where "allowed to do" could read as runtime gating.

### truthGuards — what this copy deliberately does NOT assert (for legal review)

1. **Advisory, not enforcement.** Says outright "decision guidance, not a runtime guard."
   `buildGovernanceBrief` returns recommendations; it is not in any agent's execution path.
   Enforcement (decision receipts, fail-closed execution) is the separate deeper ACGS layer
   the page says you *connect briefs to* (`Marketing.tsx:1854`) — **not** claimed here. No
   enforcement verb (block, prevent, enforce, guard) appears.
2. **Regulated items = obligations to consider, never satisfied.** GDPR/HIPAA/PCI named
   only as "obligations to consider for that task," mirroring the shipped disclaimers
   (`governance-domains.ts:60,88,105,133`). "Not legal advice" stated explicitly.
3. **Domain naming is backed by content, not decoration.** Obligations differ in substance
   per domain (gdpr Art. 22 lawful basis vs hipaa_phipa PHI minimum-necessary vs pci
   cardholder-data isolation) with distinct `weightDeltas`, wired at `Marketing.tsx:638,665`,
   rendered at `:1473–1487`.
4. **No live/hosted behavior asserted.** Copy stays inside "in your browser." No "point your
   agent at a URL," no live fetch. The URL-param ingestion (`parseDeploymentContext`) is real
   but kept out because its hosted form is ops-gated (`safeToClaimProduction = false`).
5. **Gated copy.** Not cleared to ship; nothing reads as already approved.
6. **Banned words / shipped overclaim phrases avoided.** None of *compliant, certified,
   guaranteed, production-ready, auditor-ready*; does not echo the shipped footer word
   "certification."
7. **Outcome not overstated.** Does not claim the brief prevents incidents, removes risk,
   ensures correctness, or makes an agent safe.
8. **CTA points only at the shipped in-browser interview** (`#interview`); implies no live
   host, no agent-pointed endpoint.

## Provenance & correction

The **first** run of `claim-wording-judge-panel` (`wf_9668dc42-d74`) was **contaminated by a
workflow bug**: the judge/screen stage prompts did not pin the package directory, so judge
sub-agents grepped the default working directory (the main repo on `feat/add-research-packages`,
whose `Marketing.tsx` is the older 835-line "publishing house" page) instead of the worktree.
They falsely flagged real shipped symbols (`buildGovernanceBrief`, `parseDeploymentContext`) as
"fabricated" and floored truthfulness, which corrupted the ranking and led the first
recommendation to wrongly omit the regulated-domain dimension on a stale "domain-blind" belief.

The bug was fixed (every stage prompt now pins `${PKG}` and forbids grepping cwd / live URLs),
and the workflow was re-run. Truthfulness recovered exactly as expected:

| angle | screen | truthfulness (first → corrected) |
|---|---|---|
| plain-clarity | ✅ | 8 → 9 |
| outcome-first | ❌ | **1 → 7** |
| developer-first | ❌ | **1 → 5** |
| risk-first | ✅ | 2 → 3 |

Lesson recorded at `~/.claude/projects/-home-martin-Documents-ACGS/memory/workflow-subagent-cwd-not-packagedir.md`.
